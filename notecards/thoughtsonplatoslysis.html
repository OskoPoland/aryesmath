Ignore if you are not me because I did not proofread this). 1 & 2 & 3. Bundled into the same question: Do opposites attract or are we most drawn to this similar to us. Here my perspective is entirely unfounded. I think that we are attracted to similarity, although similarity is not necessary in every aspect of life, rather the similarity must be found in the frame of mind. I think I mean that you need process the world in a similar way, that is you have a similar perspective. This doesn't mean that you understand everything the same way, instead that you come to a conclusion about something in a similar way. Because if you and your friend share the same interest/perspective/opinion but neither you nor I understand how it is that we came to this conclusion, then there is kind of nothing to latch our minds onto. I have no way of looking through the world in your eyes and you through mine. So when your thoughts change and I don't understand why we lose the thing that binds us. But if we process similarly, the thought changing, has less of an impact because I understood why it changed. 4. Is friendship always mutual? I think the answer here hinges quite heavily on whether you can truly call a one-sided friendship a friendship. Of course to be the one side in a one-sided friendship it is rather hard to understand that what you have is one-sided. But to be the other side, it is easy to dispose of the friendship label. Having been on both sides, I would not call either friendship because people are always sensitive enough to tell. It takes effort to really ignore the face that you are the one-side. There is always a nagging feeling and a hint. Shouldn't friendship be based on the unquestionable certainty that you both feel the same way about one another. Though typing this out, I see that this is probably a lot to ask for. 5. Can we expect friendship to be permanent? An interesting question because the snap answer is 'no'. Yet, of the experiences I have had with fallings out, it always seems like one party or the other comes back to 'clear the air'. Though clearing the air does not necessitate the restatement of their position as 'friend'. Instead friendships that have ended and reached a final reconcilliation open the door to a reevaluation of the relationship. Friendship may or may not remain on the table, but the option to explore is there. Though if one is to say 'yes' you must reject the idea that people can change. Permanent friendship implies that neither of you change and the way that you view one another stays stagnant or that you change along a similar enough trajectory that you remain friends. I think almost everybody has an example of a friend that is no longer a friend. 6. Is not some less exclusive form of friendship better suited to the condition and nature of man? 7.
